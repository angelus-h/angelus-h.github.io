# Paper and Materials Reference

## Overview

This reference covers paper selection, characteristics, and how surface properties shape image perception in fine art printing.

**Core Principle:** Paper is not a passive substrate—it actively shapes how an image is perceived. The same photograph on glossy RC vs. cotton rag can feel like two entirely different artworks.

---

## Paper Categories

### 1. Resin-Coated (RC) Photo Papers

**Construction:**
- Paper base coated with plastic resin layers
- Ink-receptive coating on top

**Surface Options:**
- Glossy (high sheen, maximum D-max)
- Semi-gloss/Lustre/Pearl (moderate sheen)
- Matte (no sheen)

**Characteristics:**
- **Pros:** High D-max (deep blacks), vibrant colors, affordable, widely available
- **Cons:** Plastic feel, less archival (50-75 years), "commercial" aesthetic

**Best For:**
- Commercial photography
- Vibrant color work
- Budget-conscious printing
- Test prints

**Examples:**
- Epson Premium Glossy
- Canon Pro Platinum
- Ilford Galerie Smooth Pearl

### 2. Fiber-Based (FB) Baryta Papers

**Construction:**
- Pure paper base (no plastic coating)
- Baryta layer (barium sulfate) for smooth, bright surface
- Gelatin layer for ink reception

**Characteristics:**
- **Pros:** Classic darkroom feel, excellent tonal range, high D-max, smooth surface, archival (100+ years)
- **Cons:** Expensive ($3-5 per sheet 8.5x11"), requires wide-gamut printer, can cockle if not dried flat

**Best For:**
- Black & white photography
- Dramatic landscapes (rich blacks)
- Classic photo aesthetic
- Gallery/museum prints

**Examples:**
- Hahnemühle Photo Rag Baryta 315gsm
- Canson Baryta Photographique 310gsm
- Ilford Gold Fibre Silk 310gsm

### 3. Cotton Rag Fine Art Papers

**Construction:**
- 100% cotton fiber (alpha-cellulose)
- Natural or textured surface
- Inkjet coating for pigment ink absorption

**Characteristics:**
- **Pros:** Museum-grade archival (200+ years), painterly quality, tactile texture, prestigious
- **Cons:** Lower D-max (softer blacks), high cost ($5-10 per sheet), texture can limit fine detail

**Best For:**
- Fine art photography
- Soft, atmospheric work
- Gallery exhibitions
- Limited edition prints
- Wall display (adds gravitas)

**Surface Variations:**
- Smooth (Hahnemühle Photo Rag 308gsm)
- Slight texture (Canson Platine 310gsm)
- Heavy texture (Hahnemühle William Turner 190gsm)

**Examples:**
- Hahnemühle Photo Rag 308gsm (smooth, natural white)
- Canson Infinity Platine Fibre Rag 310gsm (smooth, warm white)
- Moab Entrada Rag 290gsm (slight texture, natural white)
- Hahnemühle William Turner 190gsm (heavy watercolor texture)

## Key Paper Characteristics

### 1. Base Color (White Point)

**Bright White (Cool, ~6500K):**
- Blue-tinted, high brightness
- Effect: Modern, clinical, vibrant colors
- Examples: Epson Cold Press Bright, Hahnemühle FineArt Pearl
- Best for: High-saturation images, contemporary work, colorful subjects

**Natural White (Neutral, ~5000K):**
- Neutral, balanced white point
- Effect: Classic photographic look, versatile
- Examples: Hahnemühle Photo Rag, Canson Baryta Photographique
- Best for: Most applications, true color reproduction

**Warm White (Cream, ~4500K):**
- Yellow-tinted, soft brightness
- Effect: Nostalgic, vintage, soft mood
- Examples: Canson Platine, Moab Lasal
- Best for: Portraits, low-key work, analog emulation, intimate subjects

**Base Color Impact:**
- Cool base + warm image = color clash (avoid)
- Warm base + warm image = cohesive vintage feel (good)
- Neutral base = safe for all color palettes

**Test:** Print same neutral-toned image on bright/natural/warm papers → observe mood shift.

### 2. Surface Texture

**Smooth (≤ 10% texture):**
- Examples: Hahnemühle Photo Rag, Canson Baryta
- Pros: Maximum detail, sharp, clean rendering
- Cons: Shows banding in gradients, less forgiving of minor flaws
- Best for: High-detail subjects, sharp landscapes, portraits requiring clarity

**Slight Texture (10-30%):**
- Examples: Epson Hot Press Natural, Moab Entrada
- Pros: Hides banding, gentle softness, natural feel
- Cons: Slightly reduces fine detail
- Best for: Versatile—landscapes, portraits, general use

**Heavy Texture (30%+):**
- Examples: Hahnemühle William Turner, Canson Aquarelle Rag
- Pros: Painterly, artistic, masks imperfections (dust, scratches)
- Cons: Loses fine detail, texture can compete with image
- Best for: Abstracts, low-key work, artistic interpretations

**Texture Consideration:** View print from intended distance. Texture visible up close may disappear from 6 feet.

### 3. D-max (Maximum Black Density)

**Definition:** How deep/rich blacks can be on a given paper. Higher D-max = deeper blacks.

**High D-max (≥ 2.4):**
- Papers: Glossy RC, Canson Baryta, Hahnemühle FineArt Pearl
- Effect: Dramatic, high contrast, "gallery-worthy" blacks
- Best for: High-contrast images, dramatic landscapes, black & white

**Medium D-max (2.0-2.3):**
- Papers: Matte RC, Hahnemühle Photo Rag, Moab Entrada
- Effect: Gentle, natural, approachable blacks
- Best for: Portraits, soft-light work, neutral subjects

**Low D-max (< 2.0):**
- Papers: Heavily textured rag, watercolor papers
- Effect: Charcoal blacks, vintage/antique feel
- Best for: Fine art, low-key moody work, intentionally soft contrast

**Trade-off:** High D-max papers often feel "harder" (glossy, baryta). Low D-max papers feel "softer" (matte rag, textured).

**Test:** Print grayscale with deep blacks on 3 papers → compare black richness.

### 4. Gloss vs. Matte

**Glossy:**
- Pros: Maximum color gamut, high D-max, vibrant, sharp
- Cons: Glare under lights, fingerprints visible, "commercial" feel
- Best for: Product photography, vibrant landscapes, high-detail work

**Semi-gloss (Lustre/Pearl):**
- Pros: Compromise—good color gamut, minimal glare, versatile
- Cons: Less character than full gloss or full matte (middle-of-road)
- Best for: General purpose, when gloss is too harsh and matte too dull

**Matte:**
- Pros: No glare, elegant, fine art aesthetic, hides fingerprints
- Cons: Lower color saturation, lower D-max, can feel flat if not edited properly
- Best for: Fine art, wall display, portraits, low-key work

**Rule:** Glossy for "wow," matte for "contemplation."

### 5. Dot Gain (Ink Absorption)

**Definition:** How much ink spreads after hitting paper. High dot gain = ink absorbs/spreads more → slightly darker, softer prints.

**Low Dot Gain:**
- Papers: Glossy RC, baryta
- Effect: Sharp detail, accurate color, less ink spread
- Requires: Standard color management

**High Dot Gain:**
- Papers: Matte rag, textured papers
- Effect: Softer rendering, slightly darker prints
- Requires: Paper-specific ICC profile to compensate

**Why It Matters:** Without correct ICC profile, high dot-gain papers print too dark/muddy.

**Solution:** Always use manufacturer ICC profiles for accurate results.

## Paper Sample Library (Essential Practice)

**Goal:** Build a reference library of paper samples to guide future selections.

**Process:**
1. **Order sample packs** from 3-4 manufacturers:
 - Hahnemühle (Photo Rag, Baryta, FineArt Pearl, William Turner)
 - Canson (Baryta Photographique, Platine, Rag Photographique)
 - Epson (Hot Press Natural, Cold Press Bright)
 - Moab (Entrada, Lasal, Slickrock)

2. **Create test image** that includes:
 - Smooth gradients (sky, to test banding)
 - Fine detail (texture, to test resolution)
 - Skin tones (if portraits, to test flattering rendering)
 - Deep blacks (to test D-max)

3. **Print on 8-10 different papers** (5x7" or 8x10" size)

4. **Create reference binder** with paper samples and notes:
 - **Base color:** Cool/neutral/warm?
 - **Texture feel:** Smooth/medium/heavy?
 - **D-max impression:** Deep blacks or soft?
 - **Best use case:** Landscapes, portraits, fine art?
 - **Personal rating:** 1-5 stars

**Value:** When starting a new project, consult binder to select ideal paper based on tested results.

## Paper Matching Exercise

**Goal:** Pair images with papers that enhance their character.

**Process:**
1. Select 5 of your images with different moods:
 - **Image 1:** High-key, soft light (wedding, minimalist)
 - **Image 2:** Low-key, dramatic (moody portrait, street at night)
 - **Image 3:** Vibrant color (travel, autumn foliage)
 - **Image 4:** Neutral, documentary (street, reportage)
 - **Image 5:** Fine art, atmospheric (foggy landscape, abstract)

2. Match each to 2-3 candidate papers based on characteristics:
 - High-key → Warm white rag
 - Low-key → Baryta (deep blacks)
 - Vibrant → Bright white semi-gloss
 - Neutral → Natural white matte rag
 - Atmospheric → Textured rag, warm white

3. Print each image on its matched papers

4. Evaluate which pairing feels most cohesive:
 - Does paper enhance mood?
 - Does base color complement image palette?
 - Does texture feel appropriate?

**Expected Insight:** Right paper makes image "sing." Wrong paper creates dissonance.

## Base Color Impact Study

**Goal:** Understand how paper base color shifts image mood.

**Process:**
1. Take one neutral-toned image (grayscale or muted color)
2. Print on:
 - **Bright white base** (Epson Cold Press Bright)
 - **Natural white base** (Hahnemühle Photo Rag)
 - **Warm white base** (Canson Platine)
3. View in daylight
4. Observe:
 - Bright white: Feels clinical, modern, cooler
 - Natural white: Balanced, neutral, classic
 - Warm white: Nostalgic, vintage, warmer

**Conclusion:** Base color is a creative choice, not just technical spec.

## Printer & Paper Compatibility

### Inkjet Printer Types

**Dye-Based Ink Printers:**
- Examples: Entry-level Canon/Epson consumer models
- Pros: Vibrant color, low cost
- Cons: Poor longevity (10-20 years), not for fine art
- Best for: Proofs, test prints

**Pigment-Based Ink Printers:**
- Examples: Epson SureColor P-series, Canon imagePROGRAF PRO-series
- Pros: Archival (100+ years with proper paper), excellent D-max, wide gamut
- Cons: Higher initial cost, larger ink cartridges
- Best for: Fine art, gallery prints, archival work

**Recommended Prosumer Printers:**
- **Epson SureColor P700** (13" wide, 10-color UltraChrome Pro)
- **Epson SureColor P900** (17" wide, 10-color UltraChrome Pro)
- **Canon imagePROGRAF PRO-300** (13" wide, 10-color Lucia Pro)
- **Canon imagePROGRAF PRO-1000** (17" wide, 12-color Lucia Pro)

**Professional Large Format:**
- **Epson P5000** (17" wide, 10-color)
- **Epson P7000** (24" wide, 10-color)
- **Epson P9000** (44" wide, 10-color)

### Paper & Printer Pairing

**Wide-Gamut Printers (10+ colors):**
- Can handle any paper (glossy, matte, rag, baryta)
- Excellent color on fine art papers
- Recommended: Epson P-series, Canon PRO-series

**Standard 6-Color Printers:**
- Adequate for RC photo papers
- Limited on fine art rag (color gamut restrictions)
- May struggle with deep blacks on matte papers

**Rule:** Fine art cotton rag papers require wide-gamut pigment printers for best results.

## Archival Considerations

### Print Longevity Factors

**Ink Type:**
- Dye-based: 10-20 years (fades under light)
- Pigment-based: 100-200+ years (lightfast, archival)

**Paper Type:**
- RC: 50-75 years (plastic degrades)
- Baryta: 100+ years (fiber-based, stable)
- Cotton rag: 200+ years (museum-grade, lignin-free)

**Display Conditions:**
- Indirect light: Extends lifespan significantly
- Direct sunlight: Accelerates fading (avoid)
- UV-filtering glass: Adds protection
- Humidity control: Prevents mold, cockling

### Wilhelm Imaging Research Data

Independent testing of print permanence:
- https://www.wilhelm-research.com/

**Example Results (pigment inks, glass frame, indirect light):**
- Hahnemühle Photo Rag: 200+ years
- Canson Baryta: 150+ years
- Epson Premium Glossy RC: 75-100 years

## Key Takeaways

1. **Paper is not passive** → Actively shapes image perception, mood, and longevity.
2. **Three main categories** → RC (affordable, vibrant), Baryta (classic photo), Cotton Rag (fine art).
3. **Base color matters** → Cool/neutral/warm shifts overall image mood.
4. **Surface texture** → Smooth (detail), textured (artistic), heavy (painterly).
5. **D-max determines black richness** → High for drama, low for soft/vintage.
6. **Glossy vs. matte is aesthetic choice** → Glossy = impact, matte = contemplation.
7. **Match paper to image** → High-key → warm rag, low-key → baryta, vibrant → bright white.
8. **Build paper sample library** → Test prints on 8-10 papers to guide future selections.
9. **Pigment inks required for archival** → 100+ year lifespan on cotton rag.
10. **Wrong paper = wrong mood** → Test before committing to final prints.
